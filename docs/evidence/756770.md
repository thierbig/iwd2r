## Notes

The original reads the sprite position from `CGameObject::m_pos` directly
(this+6 / this+0xA, the `/* 0006 */ CPoint m_pos` member), not through
`GetPos`; the decompiler's `in_ECX + 6` / `in_ECX + 10` are those two dwords.
The two arguments are `CPoint*` (`ret 8`): the first is written, the second
is only read. The body stores the raw difference `target - m_pos` into the
first point, then divides `dx * 15` and `dy * 15` by the integer length.
`CUtil_Hypotenuse` (0x7E8034) is the exe's flat sqrt helper: the squared
length is loaded as a 32-bit int, converted to double, and its square root is
taken; `__ftol` (0x7E812C) truncates that double towards zero (verified at
0x756799-0x7567B3: `fild dword [esp+0x1c]`, `fstp qword [esp+0xc]`, the
qword pushed to the helper, `__ftol`). There is no +0.5 rounding.

The length is used as the divisor of an integer `idiv` at 0x7567CC and
0x7567DC. Coincident points make the squared length - and so the truncated
length - zero, and the exe raises #DE; there is no guard in the body. Its
only caller, `CGameEffectSparkle::ApplyEffect` (0x4B1700, call at 0x4B17D2),
calls only when `GetPos()` differs from `m_source` in x or y, so the game
never reaches the zero-length case through it. The trap itself is pinned in
`tests/test_sprite_actions.cpp` in a forked child (SIGFPE), not hidden behind
a guard the original does not have.

The caller treats the result as an offset, not a position: after the call it
adds the two written dwords to the `ptPos` it took from `GetPos()` (0x4B17D7
to 0x4B17F3), giving the point 15 units from the sprite towards `m_source`.
`CGameEffectSparkle::ApplyEffect` now does the same.
