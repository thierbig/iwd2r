   `durationType` 1 (the WORD at 0x8491AA) is decoded from `m_pos`, this sprite as source, and
   `CPoint(-1,-1)` as target, and sent as a `CMessageAddEffect(effect, this, this)` (vtable
   0x8487CC) (0x75BE48-0x75BECE).
5. The check value is the signed `m_derivedStats.m_nSkills[10]` (OPEN LOCK; derived stats +0x14E
   from the sprite's +0xA6E) times 7: `lea ebx,[eax*8]; sub ebx,eax` (0x75BED1-0x75BEEA). It is
   compared **unsigned** with the lock's difficulty. A local `CAITrigger(0, 0)` is built
   (0x75BEF1).
6. Door (TYPE_DOOR and `m_dwFlags & 2` - locked): the difficulty is the DWORD at +0x660
   (`CGameDoor::m_lockDifficulty`). `FeedBack(83 /*ROLL*/, check, difficulty, sub_763E40(10),
   0x9985, 0, 0)` (0x75BF28-0x75BF46), then:
   - difficulty 100: unpickable - `CAITRIGGER_PICKLOCKFAILED` (0x60) trigger message,
     `FeedBack(49 /*LOCK_UNPICKABLE*/, 0, 0, 0, -1, 0, 0)`, sound `AMB_D21` (0x75C116-0x75C26A).
   - check < difficulty (and not 100): `CAITRIGGER_PICKLOCKFAILED`, `FeedBack(9
     /*LOCKPICKFAILED*/, 0, 0, 0, -1, 0, 0)`, sound `AMB_D21` (0x75C26F-0x75C3C5).
   - otherwise the pick succeeds: `CAITRIGGER_UNLOCKED` (0x57) trigger message, the door's own
     `m_dwFlags &= ~2`, `FeedBack(8 /*LOCKPICKSUCCEEDED*/, 0, 0, 0, -1, 0, 0)`, sound
     `AMB_D21D`, then `CMessageUnlock(m_dwFlags, this, door)` (vtable 0x848C74, flags at +0xC)
     (0x75BF5D-0x75C108).
7. Container (TYPE_CONTAINER and `m_dwFlags & 1` - locked): the difficulty is the WORD at +0x88C
   (`CGameContainer::m_lockDifficulty`), the flags the DWORD at +0x88E. The same four cases and
   the same messages, with `CAITRIGGER_*`, `FeedBack(83/49/9/8, ...)`, the same sounds, and
   `CMessageUnlock(m_dwFlags & ~1, this, container)`. Unlike the door, the container's own flags
   are NOT cleared here - the unlock message's `Run` applies them (`CGameContainer::SetFlags`)
   (0x75C3CA-0x75C913).
8. ACTION_DONE (0x75C918-0x75C975); the trigger and the temporary strings are destructed.

The trigger IDs come from the words at 0x847E46 (0x57) and 0x847E58 (0x60); the messages'
vtables are 0x848C90 (CMessageSetForceActionPick), 0x847BB8 (CMessageSetTrigger), 0x848C74
(CMessageUnlock) and 0x8487CC (CMessageAddEffect). The original's branch layout reaches the
unpickable block from both arms of the `skill < difficulty` test through a shared label; the
port states it as `difficulty == 100` first, then `check < difficulty`, then success, which is
the same predicate order.

## Oracle

No oracle: the fixture would need a live game with a loaded area, a real locked door and
container, the party's sprite and the message handler. The proof is the data test (see the
exemption) on the engine in-process: the party member at AR1000_Door1 starts the walk from the
start position, then (next to it) a difficulty of 0 clears the door's locked bit and queues the
unlocked trigger and an unlock message with the bit clear; a difficulty of 1000 queues the
picklock-failed trigger and no unlock; a difficulty of 100 queues the unpickable try; and a
locked barrel queues the unlocked trigger and an unlock message that clears only the locked bit
while the container's own flags are left to the message.
