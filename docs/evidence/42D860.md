## Notes

The port is `CBaldurMessage::UpdateDemandCharacters` in `src/CMessage.cpp`, with
the new helper `DemandCharacterSlotUpdate` (0x42DF50) and a stubbed
`OnUpdateCharacterSlotReply` (0x42E630, 2,766 bytes, out of this task's scope).
It keeps the connection guard, the 0..5 vs single-slot walk, the
GetDeny/ReleaseDeny retry, the controller check against `m_nLocalPlayer`, the
progress-bar show/step/clear sequence (reason 19714, step 500000), the
controller demand and wait loop, the fallback host demand/wait loop, the
`m_bDisplayStale` flag flip (TRUE for the controller, FALSE for the host) and the
`OnUpdateCharacterSlotReply` calls.

Two quirks are reproduced: the first argument's stack slot doubles as the
"progress bar managed" flag, so the cleanup runs on every call and the bar is
only shown for a single-slot update; and the progress step runs even for a slot
whose `GetDeny` failed or that the local player controls.

Proof is the unit test
`CBaldurMessage::UpdateDemandCharacters walks the slots and drives the progress
bar` in `tests/test_network.cpp` (`[msg][network]`, no `IWD2_DATA`, with a
zeroed `CBaldurChitin`/`CInfGame` whose `m_characters` are all
`INVALID_INDEX`, so `GetDeny` answers `BAD_INDEX` before it touches the array):
the closed-session call touches nothing; update-all steps the bar by
6 * 500000; a single slot steps it once, a slot above 5 uses `INVALID_INDEX` and
still steps once; `a3 == FALSE` never steps; `m_bWaiting` is cleared. The
GetDeny-success path (controller lookup, demand send, wait and reply) needs a
live object array and a session, hence the read-only kind.

**Follow-up (box off).** `python3 tools/oracle.py evidence 0x42D860 --force`
for the Ghidra decompilation; a session fixture (a real object array entry, a
controller mapping and two players) is the only way to exercise the demand and
reply arms. Structural parity will flag the inlined CString and progress-bar
